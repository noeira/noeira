Dreamer 4 replaces [DreamerV3](/algorithms/dreamerv3/)'s recurrent RSSM with a
**transformer world model trained as a diffusion model**, and learns its policy
entirely offline — inside imagination, with no environment interaction at all.

## The change

DreamerV3 compresses each frame into a recurrent latent `(h, z)` and predicts
forward one step at a time through a GRU. Dreamer 4 replaces both halves of that:
a **causal tokenizer** turns video into continuous latents, and a **block-causal
transformer** denoises those latents under a diffusion-style objective.

<Dreamer4WorldModel />

:::note[Not a discrete-token autoregressive model]
The name suggests tokens in the language-model sense. They are not that. The
tokenizer's bottleneck is a handful of **continuous** latent tokens per frame —
a linear projection to a small channel dimension followed by `tanh` — and the
dynamics model *denoises* them rather than predicting a next token from a
codebook. There is no vector quantisation anywhere in the model.
:::

Three architectural choices carry most of the design.

**The tokenizer is trained by masked autoencoding.** Input patches are dropped
with a probability that is itself randomised per image, `p ~ U(0, 0.9)`, and
replaced by a learned embedding; the loss is reconstruction on the dropped
positions. Sometimes `p = 0`, which is the setting inference actually uses. The
paper reports that this improves the *spatial consistency* of the video the
dynamics model later generates — the tokenizer is being taught to infer missing
structure, not just to compress.

**Both halves are causal in time.** That is what lets frames be decoded one at a
time during interactive rollout, while still getting temporal compression.

**Space and time attention are separated, and time is rare.** Rather than dense
attention over all video tokens, the stack alternates three space-only layers
with one causal time layer. Time attention every fourth layer is enough, and it
is the expensive one — it is what has to read a long context's KV cache. Grouped
query attention shrinks that cache further. This is the whole reason the model
reaches real-time inference on a single GPU.

## Shortcut forcing

A flow-matching model is trained to nudge a corrupted sample a little way toward
clean data. Generating anything then needs a long chain of small steps — 64 or
more is typical — which is fine for offline video generation and hopeless for a
world model you want to imagine inside of.

<ShortcutForcing />

Shortcut forcing fixes that by conditioning the network on the **step size** it
is being asked to take, and adding a self-consistency constraint: one step of
size `d` must land where two steps of `d/2` land. The two half-steps are run
forward-only and their result is detached, so the gradient flows through the
single-step pass alone. Train that at every scale — `d` sampled as a power of
two — and the model can be asked for large steps directly at inference.

The result is **K = 4 forward passes per generated frame**.

Two refinements in the paper are worth knowing, because both look like details
and are not:

**x-prediction, not v-prediction.** Shortcut models normally predict the
velocity `x₁ − x₀`. Dreamer 4 predicts the clean representation instead.
Velocity prediction trains the network to emit high-frequency outputs, and when
you generate a long video frame by frame those errors accumulate; predicting the
endpoint gives rollouts of arbitrary length.

**A ramp weight on the loss, `w(τ) = 0.9τ + 0.1`.** At low signal levels the
flow-matching term degenerates into predicting the dataset mean — there is
almost nothing to learn there — so capacity is steered toward the levels that
carry signal.

At inference the model is autoregressive in time, and the past frames it
conditions on are deliberately corrupted slightly (`τ_ctx = 0.1`) so it stays
robust to imperfections in its own generations.

## The three phases

The agent is not trained end to end. Reading the phases in order is the fastest
way to understand what is frozen when.

| Phase | What trains | What is frozen |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · World model pretraining** | tokenizer on video, then dynamics on tokenized video (+ actions, optionally) | — |
| **2 · Agent finetuning** | task-conditioned policy and reward heads, jointly with the video-prediction loss | tokenizer |
| **3 · Imagination training** | policy and value heads only, on rollouts the model generates | the whole transformer |

**Phase 2 inserts agent tokens** as a new modality, interleaved with the image
representations, actions and register tokens, and carrying a task embedding. The
policy and reward heads read from them, trained by multi-token prediction — each
head predicts the next `L = 8` actions and rewards rather than only the next one.

:::tip[Agent tokens are attended *from*, never *to*]
Agent tokens attend to themselves and to every other modality. No other modality
attends back to them.

This asymmetry is load-bearing. If the world model could see the agent tokens,
its future predictions could be influenced by the *task* rather than only by the
actions — causal confusion, and it would quietly stop being a world model. The
one-way mask is what keeps prediction honest.
:::

**Phase 3 keeps the transformer frozen** and updates only the policy and value
heads, so imagination training cannot damage the world model it depends on. A
frozen copy of the phase-2 policy is kept as a behavioural prior. Rollouts start
from dataset contexts, one rollout per context — a deliberate change from earlier
Dreamers, trading rollout depth for data diversity and lower memory.

The policy objective is **PMPO**, not the usual advantage-weighted update. It
uses only the **sign** of the advantage, splitting imagined states into
positive and negative sets and averaging a maximum-likelihood loss over each,
balanced by `α = 0.5`, plus a reverse-KL pull toward the behavioural prior at
`β = 0.3`. Discarding the magnitude is the point: it removes any need to
normalise returns or advantages, which is what lets one set of hyperparameters
cover tasks whose reward scales differ by orders of magnitude.

The value head is trained on λ-returns with a symexp-twohot output, the same
distributional trick [DreamerV3](/algorithms/dreamerv3/) and
[TD-MPC2](/algorithms/tdmpc2/) use, and for the same reason.

## Status

:::caution[Known open issue: the perceptual loss corrupts reconstruction]
The paper's tokenizer loss is `MSE + 0.2 · LPIPS`. LPIPS is a *learned*
perceptual metric, and this port supplies it through a surrogate trained on
out-of-domain data — which turns out to corrupt token reconstruction rather than
improve it.

The working direction is to set the perceptual weight to zero and pre-train the
tokenizer separately before joint training. Since the tokenizer is frozen for
every later phase, nothing downstream can be better than it is.

There is also an operational trap — a stale online checkpoint must be deleted
before re-running, or the run resumes from the corrupted tokenizer and the fix
appears not to work.
:::

Beyond that, two Mojo-level issues have been hit here: compiler aborts on traits
refining `Module` in this package, which constrain how the model can be
structured.

## Running it

```bash
pixi run -e nvidia mojo run -I . examples/car_racing/dreamer4_car_racing_offline.mojo
pixi run -e nvidia mojo run -I . examples/car_racing/dreamer4_car_racing_online.mojo
pixi run -e nvidia mojo run -I . examples/car_racing/dreamer4_tokenizer_probe.mojo
```

The tokenizer probe is the right entry point: if reconstruction is bad, nothing
downstream can work, and the probe isolates that.

## Dreamer 4 or DreamerV3?

|  | [DreamerV3](/algorithms/dreamerv3/) | Dreamer 4 |
|---|---|---|
| Latent | recurrent `(h, z)` through a GRU | continuous tokens from a causal tokenizer |
| Dynamics | one-step prediction | diffusion, K = 4 passes per frame |
| Attention | — | space every layer, causal time every fourth |
| Policy objective | actor-critic with normalised returns | PMPO, sign of advantage only |
| Data | online interaction | offline video, actions optional |
| Status here | full Atari Pong reproduction | open issue in the tokenizer |

The offline column is the interesting one. Dreamer 4's world model learns
mostly from **unlabelled** video and needs only a small amount of
action-paired data to pick up action conditioning — which is what makes the
Minecraft-from-offline-data result possible, and what would make the approach
applicable to robotics, where online interaction with a partly-trained policy is
unsafe.

## Should you use it?

No — use [DreamerV3](/algorithms/dreamerv3/), which has a full Atari Pong
reproduction behind it.

Dreamer 4 is here because the project is developed in the open and the work is
in progress. It is documented so the state is knowable, not because it is ready.

## Related work

The same territory is being explored from another direction in
`noeira/experimental/lewm/` — a reference-exact port of LeWM, a JEPA-style
world model with no reconstruction loss, which matches the published PushT
checkpoint against PyTorch and is being trained on the SO-101 rig. It is
research code with no stability guarantee at all.
